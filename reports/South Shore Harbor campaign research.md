# Verify Coaxum's lease before pitching South Shore Harbor

**Bottom line:** the pitch is built on a real, interesting venue, but several of its load-bearing premises do not survive contact with the public record. The man introduced as "Henry Coulson" is almost certainly **Henry L. Coaxum Jr.**, a New Orleans McDonald's franchisee whose Coaxum Enterprises, Inc. is the counterparty in lakefront lease negotiations and who is also **Chairman of Nola Bank**, the proposed title sponsor. That makes the lead sponsor a related party, not an independent buyer of media. Nola Bank opened only on Sept. 14, 2026 with $29.2M in capital, so a $100K commitment is a stretch and the "sponsor validation" the plan implies is weaker than it sounds. Lamar Advertising's $2.27B revenue figure checks out, but its giving pattern is in-kind media to national charities, and "Jay Johnson" is its CFO, not a local contact. The "underutilized" framing, the Cleveland Avenue tie, the parking-lot concert and the hotel idea have no public support at all. The $5,000 starter is realistic for a one-day shoot. The path to $300K-$500K projects funded by $100K sponsors is aggressive and depends on facts (lease terms, curfew, audience data) nobody has produced yet. **Evidence caveat:** nearly everything below comes from search-result snippets, because bankingdive.com, bizneworleans.com, ir.lamar.com, neworleans.com, Substack, nolaeast.com, the Louisiana boards-and-commissions site and others were blocked. Treat all findings as medium or low confidence until checked against primaries; the highest-confidence items are flagged.

## The venue is real but the "Coulson" in the brief is almost certainly Coaxum

Searches for "Coulson" tied to South Shore Harbor, the Lakefront Management Authority (LMA) or McDonald's returned no matching person. Every source that connects a Henry to this property says **Coaxum**. Lakefront Management Authority agendas from Aug. 25, 2022 and Jan. 2023 name Coaxum Enterprises, Inc. as the counterparty for negotiating a lease of Parcel "L" ([LMA Aug 2022 agenda](https://wwwcfprd.doa.louisiana.gov/boardsandcommissions/MeetingNotices/547_REVISED%20Regular%20Board%20Meeting%20Agenda_AUGUST%2025%202022%20.pdf), [LMA Jan 2023 agenda](https://wwwcfprd.doa.louisiana.gov/BoardsAndCommissions/MeetingNotices/547_JAN%2026%202023%20Regular%20Board%20Meeting%20Agenda__FINAL(004)%2001.23.2023.pdf)). A November 2024 flood-authority update says Henry Coaxum, who leases the former Bally's site, plans to open a bar ([SLFPA-E minutes](https://www.floodauthority.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/2024-11-21-SLFPA-E-Board-Meeting-Minutes.pdf)). A WGNO item quotes Karen Coaxum, "We want this to be the destination on Lake Pontchartrain," about a venue Henry and Karen Coaxum are building on the old Bally's site ([Yahoo/WGNO](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/international-arts-festival-set-sept-224850583.html)). Confidence that "Coulson" means Coaxum: **high but not certain**, and the client should confirm the spelling before any name goes in a deck. Henry L. Coaxum Jr.'s public profile is substantial: he owns and operates McDonald's restaurants in New Orleans (sources conflict on whether it is 3 or 7), founded Coaxum Enterprises in 2002 as the management arm, started as a McDonald's manager trainee in 1984, and chaired the New Orleans Business Alliance under Mayor Landrieu ([Biz New Orleans](https://bizneworleans.com/hes-loving-it/), [LRA blog](https://lrablogs.blogspot.com/2014/07/lra-member-henry-coaxum-jr-recipient-of.html)). This is a credible local operator with civic standing, which is useful for the pitch, but it also means he is a public figure whose name you cannot afford to misspell.

The property itself is public land. South Shore Harbor Marina sits at 6701 Stars and Stripes Blvd beside Lakefront Airport, on former levee-district land managed by the LMA, successor to the Non-Flood Protection Asset Management Authority, which also runs Orleans Marina about six miles west ([Marinas in New Orleans](https://marinasinneworleans.com/leases/application), [Murray Yacht Sales](https://murrayyachtsales.com/marina-master-plan-report-lakefront-management-authority/)). The marina has roughly **447-453 open slips** plus 26 covered, though sources conflict ([Edgewater Resources](https://www.edgewaterresources.com/project/south-shore-harbor/), [Marinas.com](https://marinas.com/view/marina/16c5pvz_South_Shore_Harbor_Marina_New_Orleans_LA_United_States)). The event-relevant piece is the **19-acre former Bally's Casino site** at 6901 Stars and Stripes, now branded "The Public Market at South Shore Harbor" ([NOLA East](https://nolaeast.com/events/SOUTH%20SHORE%20HARBOR/south_shore_harbor_complex.htm)). That brand matters: the pitch should be for the Public Market and harbor, and the client likely does not control the marina slips or the marina-side Parcel L unless the lease says so.

## The ownership chain is murky, and the lease is the whole ballgame

Nobody owns the land the way the call may have implied. The ex-Bally's site is levee-district land leased to a private party, and the pitch's feasibility depends on what that lease allows. The history is: in 2015 Tipitina's owner Roland von Kurnatowski won a 50-year lease (with a 49-year option) for a roughly $12-13M festival park with an amphitheater, a fuel dock, restaurants and bars, with a commitment of at least $6M to renovate the abandoned terminal ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2015/11/27/tipitinas-owner-plans-12m-lakefront-festival-park/), [OffBeat](https://www.offbeat.com/news/tiptinas-owner-agrees-develop-new-orleans-lakefront-property)). City Council approved a 5,000-person venue ([NOLA East](https://nolaeast.com/events/SOUTH%20SHORE%20HARBOR/south_shore_harbor_complex.htm)). The project operated as Lakeshore Landing, with "Hard Hat" concerts and a bar ([OffBeat](https://www.offbeat.com/news/lakeshore-landing-hard-hat-concerts/)). Somewhere between then and 2022 the site changed hands: in 2022 the LMA ran an RFP for Parcel L (proposals due Apr. 29, 2022; special meeting June 22, 2022 to select a developer), and Coaxum Enterprises got negotiation authority that August. A January 2023 agenda includes a **motion to rescind** that authorization, and the outcome is not found ([public notice](https://www.centralauctionhouse.com/rfp25047351-request-for-proposalsqualifications-for-the-lease-of-parcel-l-located-at-south-shore-harbor-marina-new-orleans-la.html), [LMA June 2022 agenda](https://wwwcfprd.doa.louisiana.gov/boardsandcommissions/MeetingNotices/547_SPECIAL%20BOARD%20MEETING%20AGENDA%20-%20JUNE%2022%2022022.pdf)). My inference, not a sourced fact, is that Parcel L (marina side) and the 6901 site are different parcels, so the 2024 description of Coaxum "leasing the former Bally's site" may rest on a different lease than the 2022-23 negotiation. Confidence on the current lease structure: **low**. Lease term, rent, acreage, expiry and permitted uses were not found, and neither were Secretary of State filings.

Two further facts raise the stakes. First, a 2016 LMA board summary references a **10:00 p.m. closing hour** in the South Shore Harbor lease and neighbor comments ([LA DOA minutes](https://wwwcfprd.doa.louisiana.gov/BoardsAndCommissions/MeetingMinutes/547_Board%20Minutes%209-22-2016%20SUMMARY.pdf)); this is a partial excerpt about an earlier lease, but a rave concept collides with it unless the current lease differs. Second, Louisiana Act 83 (2024) requires RFP selection under a cooperative endeavor agreement for certain Orleans and Jefferson parcels ([legis.la.gov](https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=1358032)); whether it touches this site is unconfirmed. A 2024 Edgewater redevelopment study and an October 2024 Marina Master Plan show the LMA is actively planning the area, so a pitch that implies a hotel or major construction needs landlord buy-in that does not appear to exist yet. Do not repeat the OffBeat allegation that the von Kurnatowskis lost Tipitina's over fraud claims; it is a single-source snippet with no outcome ([OffBeat](https://www.offbeat.com/news/tiptinas-owner-agrees-develop-new-orleans-lakefront-property)).

## The market is crowded at the top and open at the bottom

South Shore Harbor currently runs a thin, ticketed-event program. The International Arts Festival moved to the Public Market for Sept. 24-27, 2026, with Saturday gates at 2 PM and tickets of about **$55.70-$145.97** ([Showpass](https://www.showpass.com/international-arts-fest-nola-2026-saturday/), [MyNewOrleans](https://www.myneworleans.com/international-arts-festival-to-return-in-2026/)), and a WNOZ Smooth Jazz Jam with Nils is set indoors for Nov. 7, 2026 ([Showpass](https://www.showpass.com/wnoz-smooth-jazz-jam-2/)). The venue's bar and grill is reported open for private parties ([NOLA Lakefront](https://nolalakefront.com/news/south-shore-harbor-bar-grill-is-now-open/)). JamBase shows no upcoming events, though that page may be stale ([JamBase](https://www.jambase.com/venue/south-shore-harbor)). So "underutilized" is a defensible interpretation of a large lot with occasional events, but no source uses the word or measures utilization.

On demand, the macro picture is strong: New Orleans drew **19.46M visitors and $10.8B in spending in 2025** (via search summary of [New Orleans & Company](https://www.neworleans.com/articles/post/new-orleans-company-releases-strong-visitation-results-for-2025-1946-million-visitors-spent-10b/)), Jazz Fest 2026 drew a reported 475,000 ([Biz New Orleans](https://bizneworleans.com/jazz-fest-2026-draws-475000-fans/)), and the College Football Playoff title game lands at the Superdome on Jan. 24, 2028 ([CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/college-football/news/college-football-playoff-locations-new-orleans-set-to-host-cfp-national-championship-in-2028)). For the EDM concept, the benchmark is We Belong Here, Nov. 13-15, 2026 at Goldring Woldenberg Riverfront Park (ODESZA, Carl Cox), with resale around **$95-$385** ([Techno Airlines](https://www.technoairlines.com/festival/we-belong-here-new-orleans-usa-2026), [SeatGeek](https://seatgeek.com/we-belong-here-music-festival-tickets/new-orleans)), and the National Fried Chicken Festival (Dillon Francis, A-Trak) already programs dance acts on the lakefront ([EDM.com](https://edm.com/events/dillon-francis-a-trak-djing-new-orleans-national-fried-chicken-festival/)). A small waterfront rave is therefore competing for the same audience that has just been served by a downtown festival with international headliners; sunset or early-evening formats fit both the curfew risk and that competitive reality. Age policy for We Belong Here is reported inconsistently as 18+ or 21+, which matters for how you structure alcohol sponsorship.

Weddings and hotels are the weakest-supported ideas. No New Orleans-specific wedding average exists in the sources; the national figure is about $36,000 (Zola via [Axios](https://www.axios.com/local/new-orleans/2025/02/14/2025-wedding-trends-louisiana-prices-gifts)), and downtown venue rentals run roughly $2,000-$7,000 at NOAC and from $6K at the Presbytere ([Here Comes The Guide](https://www.herecomestheguide.com/wedding-venues/louisiana/noac-events), [Wedding Spot](https://www.wedding-spot.com/venue/8848/the-presbyt%c3%a8re/)); these are aggregator listings that may be outdated. The lakefront can sell water views and proximity to Lakefront Airport, which saw roughly 1,200 private jets projected for Super Bowl LIX ([Fox 8](https://www.fox8live.com/2025/01/31/lakefront-airport-gears-up-super-bowl-lixs-vip-influx/)), but no data links jet traffic to wedding demand. No hotel plan at this site appears anywhere in the research. On the distribution side, "The Nola Collective" exists as a New Orleans events Substack with weekly "TNCweekend" guides ([Substack](https://thenolacollective.substack.com/p/welcome)), but no subscriber count, social following, founder or "Bianca" connection could be verified because the page fetch was blocked. Until the client supplies reach numbers, treat it as an unproven channel; micro-community partners often out-engage large accounts per post (Reels engagement benchmarks run roughly 0.5-1.5% depending on source, per [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/data-geeks/instagram_benchmarks_2025.pdf) and [Searchlab](https://searchlab.nl/en/statistics/instagram-statistics-2026)), but that is a general pattern, not evidence about this collective.

## The sponsors are weaker and more entangled than the call suggested

**Nola Bank** is real and the call was right that a new bank would want local visibility, but the context cuts against a $100K cash title deal. It received final approval Sept. 11, opened Sept. 14, 2026, is Louisiana's first new bank in about 16 years, and raised **$29.2M** in capital ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/new-orleans-de-novo-nola-bank-community-mdi-fdic/830889/), [Biz New Orleans](https://bizneworleans.com/nola-bank-opens-as-states-1st-in-16-years/)). It operates from a temporary facility at 1100 S. Carrollton Ave. with no branches, and its stated mission is serving the underbanked and small businesses ([NewOrleans.com](https://www.neworleans.com/articles/post/nola-bank-opens-in-new-orleans-as-louisianas-first-new-bank-since-2010/)). The detail the call apparently did not surface: **its Chairman is Henry L. Coaxum, Jr.**, the same person who leases the venue. Whether he is also a shareholder or the person steering the sponsorship is unknown, but any sponsorship from Nola Bank is a related-party deal. That is not disqualifying, but it changes what the deal proves: it is not market validation, a second sponsor reading the venue's pitch might ask about it, and regulators scrutinize insider-related spending at a brand-new bank. I found no record of Nola Bank sponsoring any event in 2026. For scale, $100K is about 0.34% of its raised capital, which is not impossible, but capital is not a marketing budget and I found no data on the latter. Total assets, MDI status and any marketing contact are unverified.

**Lamar Advertising** is real and large: FY2025 net revenue was about **$2.27B** (up about 2.7% from $2.21B), net income $593.1M, headquartered in Baton Rouge with 363,000+ displays and a New Orleans market page ([Lamar IR](https://ir.lamar.com/node/14276/pdf), [Digital Signage Pulse](https://digitalsignagepulse.com/news/lamar-advertising-company-q4-and-full-2025-year-results/), [Lamar New Orleans](https://lamar.com/neworleans)). That figure checks out. But revenue is not sponsorship capacity: Lamar is a rent-the-space business, and its documented giving is in-kind media, such as a yearly pro bono partner (Make-A-Wish above $1M in media, Boys & Girls Clubs in 2024, Ronald McDonald House in 2026 with more than $2M of digital out-of-home) and a Community Support program donating space to nonprofits and law enforcement ([Lamar BGCA release](https://lamar.com/en/about/news/2024/lamar-boys-girls-clubs-of-america-partnership), [Media4Growth](https://www.media4growth.com/ooh-industry/company-news/lamar-advertising-and-ronald-mcdonald-house-enter-into-yearlong-pro-bono-partnership-83498)). "Jay Johnson" matches **Jay L. Johnson, Lamar's CFO** since Oct. 1, 2019, with no New Orleans tie found ([CapEdge 8-K](https://capedge.com/filing/899045/0001193125-19-161361/8K)); his current status in November 2026 was not re-verified. If the client has a personal relationship with the CFO, that is an access point, but the decision on a local event would normally sit with the New Orleans market team. Realistically, expect remnant billboard or digital space, which vendor estimates price at roughly $1,200-$3,500 per surface-street unit and $2,500-$8,000 on interstate ([AdQuick](https://www.adquick.com/billboard-locations/louisiana/new-orleans)), and note that in-kind valued at rate card is not cash against a production budget.

For context, New Orleans sponsor money clusters in established properties with audience data: Shell and Ochsner at Jazz Fest, First Horizon as its official bank, Chevron as a 13-year French Quarter Fest title sponsor now exiting that title role, and Entergy at the Bayou Classic ([Biz New Orleans](https://bizneworleans.com/jazz-fest-2026-draws-475000-fans/), [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/1118881/first-horizon-bank-and-new-orleans-jazz-heritage-festival-celebrate-11-years-of-partnership-new-first-horizon-lounge-debuts-near-main-stage-for-cardholders), [Business Report](https://www.businessreport.com/?p=270214), [Entergy](https://www.entergy.com/entergy-sponsors-51st-annual-bayou-classic-expands-partnerships-with-hbcus)). No dollar amounts are public. Industry commentary says sponsors want measurable outcomes and bespoke packages and that year-one events do better with fewer, deeper partners ([FestivalPro](https://festivalpro.com/festival-management/4616/news/2026/1/14/Strategies-for-Handling-Increased-Competition-for-Music-Festival-Sponsors.html), [Ticket Fairy](https://branded.ticketfairy.com/blog/fewer-sponsors-deeper-partnerships-sponsorship-strategies-for-year-one-festivals)). The IEG figure of $169M+ in rights fees across 39 major U.S. music festivals works out to about $4.3M each by my arithmetic, dominated by large festivals, so it overstates mid-size economics ([IEG](https://sponsorship.com/2025/11/18/2025-music-festivals/)).

## The plan scales only if the starter proves audience and the money math is rebuilt

The $5,000 starter is credible as a lean package. Vendor sources put small-to-mid crew days at $800-$4,000 and a three-to-five-person team at $2,500-$8,000 per day, with a typical 60-second commercial video averaging about $3,213 (range $1,000-$16,000) ([Levitate Media](https://levitatemedia.com/learn/video-production-costs-explained-what-you-need-to-know), [Viva Media](https://vivamedia.ca/blog/corporate-video-pricing), [Advids](https://advids.co/pricing/how-much-commercial-corporate-video-creation-cost)). That buys roughly one shoot day and a few edited cuts, not talent, music licensing, drone permits or paid media. These are vendor-published, national numbers, so confidence is medium.

The jump to **$300K-$500K projects** is where the plan strains. A single $100K commitment fully covers an $80K production but only a tenth of a $1M one; funding a $1M budget purely from $100K sponsors implies ten of them, which is aggressive for a year-one venue with no attendance history, and industry guidance favors fewer, deeper deals. Two of the likely first sponsors are a related-party bank and a media company that gives in-kind, so the real cash pool could be far smaller than "$100K per sponsor" implies. Public funding does not close the gap: Louisiana's tourism competitive grant has historically capped near $10,000 and is reimbursed after the event with a 90-day lead time ([CGP FY25 guide](https://www.explorelouisiana.com/sites/default/files/2024-11/CGP%20FY25%20Application%20Guide%20FINAL%20with%20Video.docx)); the Event Incentive Fund needs applications 180 days out with amounts not found ([Major Events application](https://www.explorelouisiana.com/sites/default/files/2023-09/Major%20Events%20Application_20230925.pdf)); and the 40% film credit under Act 44 is aimed at qualifying productions, with eligibility for branded event content unconfirmed ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/tax-authorities/1636658/governor-landry-signs-act-44-to-modify-the-film-production-tax-credit-program)). Event execution also needs layered permits: a city special-event permit, temporary alcohol permits at city and state level, an occupational license and a bond or sales-tax deposit ([City Special Event Guide](https://www.nola.gov/nola/media/One-Stop-Shop/Office%20of%20Cultural%20Economy/Special-Event-Guide.pdf)), plus landlord consent. The case studies are thin: Baltimore's Inner Harbor claims $2.3B in activity but is a different scale ([IMPLAN](https://www.casestudies.com/company/implan/case-study/harboring-tourism-reinvesting-in-baltimores-inner-harbor)), and a Virginia marina's attendance grew while revenue fell, a warning that traffic without monetization is not a business ([WY Daily](https://wydaily.com/latest/local/2019/09/23/marina-attendance-grows-but-revenue-drops-heres-what-the-county-plans-to-do-about-it/)). My overall read: treat the $5K phase as a low-risk paid test whose real output is audience data, a lease clarification and a sponsor prospectus, and refuse to commit to the $300K-$500K tier until those exist.

## Open questions to ask the client and at the site visit

Ask first about identity and authority: confirm the correct spelling and full name of the operator, whether he is Henry L. Coaxum Jr., and whether "Harry Coaxum" at Cleveland Avenue is the same person, a relative or someone else. Ask who holds the lease on the 6901 Stars and Stripes site and Parcel L, and request the executed lease, including term, rent, permitted uses, curfew or noise terms, alcohol rights, sponsorship and naming rights, and whether the LMA must approve events and sponsors. Ask what happened with the January 2023 motion to rescind and whether the Public Market is complete or still under construction.

On sponsors, ask what Coaxum's role is at Nola Bank (chairman, shareholder, decision-maker on marketing spend) and whether the bank has approved any budget, who at Nola Bank owns marketing, whether the sponsorship has been discussed with the board or its regulators, and who the Lamar contact actually is and whether the offer is in-kind or cash. Ask whether any commitment is in writing, since "$100K commitments" on a call carry no weight until they are. On the audience, ask for The Nola Collective's subscriber and social numbers, the real name and role of "Bianca," and past campaign results; ask what event history, attendance, ticket sales and demographics the Public Market has from IAF and the jazz jam. At the site, check which parking lot the concert idea refers to, capacity, power, restrooms, traffic and parking flows, proximity to residents, and what the Lakefront Airport neighbor allows. Ask what production budgets of $80K-$1M refer to, who pays, and what the starter $5,000 is expected to deliver and who owns the footage and rights.

## Recommended next steps

First, resolve identity and authority before anything else. Get the lease or a written authorization letter from the lessee and confirm the LMA's role. Second, pull the primaries that were blocked: the LMA board minutes (June 26 and Oct. 23, 2025, plus 2026), Louisiana Secretary of State filings for Coaxum Enterprises, Nola Bank and any South Shore Harbor entity, the FDIC BankFind record for Nola Bank, Lamar's investor-relations page for current officers, and the Nola Collective Substack and social accounts. Third, check the real constraints: the current curfew and noise terms, city special-event permit requirements via specialevents@nola.gov (the guide says older intake channels are closed), insurance minimums, and alcohol licensing. Fourth, restructure the commercial offer: price the $5,000 phase as a defined deliverable (one day, a stated number of cuts, usage rights), ask that sponsors sign letters of intent before any larger scope, and model the $300K-$500K tier on realistic sponsor counts, in-kind versus cash splits and a 6-12 month sales cycle (an industry rule of thumb I could not source). Fifth, disclose the related-party relationship to any other prospective sponsor so it is not discovered later, and prefer an event concept that fits the likely 10 p.m. constraint, such as a sunset series, over a late-night rave. Finally, drop the Cleveland Avenue, hotel and "underutilized" claims from outward-facing material until sourced, and name the venue in pitch material as "The Public Market at South Shore Harbor" where that is the space in question.

## What changes after this research

The call framed South Shore Harbor as an open canvas with a well-connected operator and two corporate sponsors waiting. The record shows something more constrained and more interesting: a public-land venue with a long history of ambitious but unfinished plans, an operator who also chairs the proposed lead sponsor, and a lease that the pitch cannot be built on until someone reads it. The most valuable output of the first $5,000 is not content; it is audience data and written clarity on rights, because those are what convert a friendly call into sponsors who pay.

*Confidence note: the findings on Coaxum's lease role, Nola Bank's chairmanship and opening, Lamar's revenue and CFO are supported by multiple search snippets and are the most reliable. Findings on lease terms, the curfew, sponsor intent, The Nola Collective and weddings are low confidence or absent. No full page was read for most sources.*
